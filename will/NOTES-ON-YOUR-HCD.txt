NOTES ON YOUR ADVANCE HEALTH CARE DIRECTIVE (51 state documents)
==================================================================

WHERE THE WORDS CAME FROM
Your "04_AHCD/FINAL DOCUMENT FOR CODER" folder has a national baseline plus one controlled document per
state (the "V1/V2/V3 Audited" files; where both a V2 and a V3 exist for a state, I used the V3 -- the two
are nearly identical, V3 is the newer revision). I also found an existing prototype at
"CODER/LAST WILL PACKAGE/Grapevine_Last_Will_and_Testament_Package_V27/powers_engine/hcd_templates.js"
that already had all 51 of those documents converted into paragraph-by-paragraph data, along with the
county lists used for the dropdown (see below). I checked that data against your source .docx files for
several states (Alabama, California, Texas, New York, Florida, Ohio) word for word -- it matched almost
exactly, so I used it as the starting point instead of re-typing 51 documents by hand, the way I did for
the power of attorney. Every state's full text, including every statutory notice, is preserved.

WHY THIS ONE STAYS IN THIRD PERSON ("the Principal"), UNLIKE YOUR OTHER DOCUMENTS
Your will, power of attorney, and dementia directive all speak as "I." I kept this document as your source
documents wrote it -- "the Principal" -- rather than converting it, because much of this text is not just
Grapevine's own drafting: it reproduces state-required statutory notices and, in some states (California,
Texas, and others), the state's own official form language word for word. Changing the pronouns on text
that is meant to match a statutory form is a bigger judgment call than the will or the dementia directive,
so I left it as your documents have it. Say the word if you'd like it converted to "I" for consistency, and
I'll do it the same careful way, sentence by sentence.

A DROPDOWN FOR STATE AND COUNTY, AS YOU ASKED
The county list (all 3,143 counties and county-equivalents -- parishes in Louisiana, boroughs in Alaska,
independent cities in Virginia, and so on) is in will/counties.js. Picking a state fills the county dropdown
from that state's real counties. This also let me improve on how your documents were drafted: about 22 of
the 51 states have a "County of ____________________" blank in their notary or acknowledgment section. I
filled that blank in from the person's answer instead of leaving it blank -- something your source
documents didn't do (they just left it as a line to fill in by hand). The other ~29 states either don't
need a county in the signing section, or ask for it in a longer sentence I left as a blank to be safe rather
than risk filling in the wrong spot.

TWO PROBLEMS I FOUND AND FIXED IN YOUR SOURCE DOCUMENTS
1. Alabama's Article Seven (Artificial Nutrition and Hydration) had a duplicated, partly-rewritten clause --
   two nearly-identical paragraphs both starting "[[IF: ...WITHHOLD_WITHDRAW]]" back to back, immediately
   followed by a third, properly-structured version of the same choice. I checked this against your actual
   .docx file (Grapevine_HCD_Alabama_V3_Audited_Notices.docx) and the duplication is there in the source,
   not just in the converted data. I kept the properly-structured version and removed the two duplicates,
   keeping every sentence's wording exactly as your document had it.
2. Two states have a small grammar slip, the same slip in the same two sentences: Texas says "a Agent"
   (should be "an Agent") and the District of Columbia says "a Attorney" (should be "an Attorney") --
   both states use "Agent"/"Attorney in Fact" instead of "Health Care Agent" as their defined term, and it
   looks like whatever process substituted that term in missed fixing "a" to "an" in two spots. I fixed
   just the article ("a" to "an"), nothing else, in both states.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Every token in your controlled documents is asked about: the health care agent and backups, life-sustaining
treatment, artificial nutrition, artificial hydration, anatomical gifts, and three optional pieces of
guidance (values, limits on your agent, anything else). Nothing was shortened here the way the power of
attorney and the dementia directive were -- you asked this one to reflect each state's requirements, so it
asks everything your documents call for. That includes state-specific questions that appear ONLY for the
state where they matter:
  California   -- currently a patient or resident in a skilled nursing facility (adds a required witness
                  statement from a patient advocate or ombudsman)
  Connecticut  -- resident of a DMHAS facility; resident of a DDS facility; what happens if pregnant
                  (three-way choice, with room to write your own instructions)
  Arizona      -- physically unable to sign your own name; authority for inpatient psychiatric admission;
                  authority to arrange funeral and disposition
  Nebraska     -- require a second physician to confirm incapacity
  Ohio         -- Ohio's own express, separate authorization for withdrawing nutrition/hydration if
                  permanently unconscious (Ohio law requires this to be asked separately from the general
                  choice, with the Principal's initials)
South Carolina's document includes an optional notary section that the document itself says is "not
required to create a valid health care power of attorney" -- I always include it rather than asking a
question, since your own document treats it as freely available, not conditional on a preference.

SIGNING METHOD
16 of your 51 states offer more than one way to sign (two witnesses, or a notary, or in a few states a
justice of the peace or a specific acknowledgment format); the builder asks which one only in those states,
and uses your document's own witness or notary wording for whichever is chosen. The other states have one
method built into their document, so nothing is asked.

WHAT I DID NOT DO
I did not attempt the standalone "no AHCD" scenario for the dementia directive's own 51 state execution
routes -- see will/NOTES-ON-YOUR-DEMENTIA.txt, which already flagged that the real per-state wording for
those routes appears to live in this project, not in the dementia-directive files. Now that this Health
Care Directive builder exists with real per-state execution text, that connection could be made -- ask if
you'd like that done.

HOW TO EDIT
Folder: will/hcd/          the full text of each state's directive, one file per state
File:   will/hcd-template.js   the "sample" watermark switch (there's no shared body text to edit here)
File:   will/hcd-signing.js    the signing steps
File:   will/counties.js       the state and county lists used for the dropdowns
The notes at the top of each state file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[elif y=VALUE]], [[each successors]]).
