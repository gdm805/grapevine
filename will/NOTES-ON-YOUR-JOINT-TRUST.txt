NOTES ON YOUR JOINT REVOCABLE LIVING TRUST (married couple / domestic partners)
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
This is the joint route from your file
Grapevine_Coder_Ready_V12_Joint_RLT_All_Section_Numbers_Fully_Corrected.docx -- for two people
(a married couple or registered domestic partners) creating one trust together. It reuses the
same architecture as your single-trustmaker trust (will/trust-template.js): one shared,
state-agnostic document, plus the same state-by-state signing page.

HOW THIS DOCUMENT IS BUILT
Same pattern as the single trust: one shared document file (will/trust-joint-template.js) plus
the same state-by-state signing page used by both trusts (will/trust-state-text.js), spliced in
with [[include trust_execution]]. Florida and Delaware need two witnesses in addition to a
notary; every other state and DC need a notary only. The Florida and Delaware witness wording
says "the Trustmaker" (singular) in your source file, since it's shared with the single trust --
for the joint trust it now says "the Trustmakers" (plural), switched automatically based on
which builder is running.

YOU ASKED FOR 30 PAGES OR LESS -- WHAT'S IN, AND WHAT'S LEFT OUT
Same four features cut as the single trust, for the same reasons (each is either advanced,
rarely used, or belongs to a bigger project than this pass):
  - The Companion Animal Trust (a dedicated sub-trust just for a pet).
  - The Charitable Gifts sub-section (special handling when a named charity no longer exists).
  - The Beneficiary Trust option for "everything else" -- everyone named for the remainder of
    your trust receives their share outright, the way your will already works.
  - The "Independent Trustee" definition, which only mattered for the Beneficiary Trust option
    above -- cut along with it so no defined term is left referring to a feature that isn't here.
Two more simplifications specific to the joint trust:
  - The Family Trust's power of appointment: your source document offers the surviving spouse or
    partner a choice between a "Fixed Descendant Distribution" (the Family Trust goes to fixed,
    named beneficiaries) and a "Limited Power of Appointment" (the survivor can redirect it among
    a group at their death). This version keeps the Fixed Descendant Distribution only -- your
    named beneficiaries for the Family Trust are fixed and can't be changed by the survivor.
  - First-death funding: your document offers a choice between funding the Family Trust directly
    or funding it through a disclaimer (letting the survivor decide, after the first death,
    whether to disclaim property into it for tax reasons). This version uses direct funding only.
Say the word if you'd like the Limited Power of Appointment or disclaimer funding added back in.

CHILDREN ARE SIMPLIFIED TO ONE COMBINED LIST
Your source document separately models joint children, children of only one of you, and blended
families, with different distribution language for each combination. This version asks for one
combined list of children and treats all of them the same way under the trust. If you have a
blended family and want children treated differently depending on which of you is their parent,
tell us and we'll build that out as a real option.

SPECIFIC GIFTS ARE ONE COMBINED LIST PER DEATH, NOT SPLIT BY WHO DIES FIRST
Your source document asks for a separate list of specific gifts depending on which of you dies
first (since either of you could be first). This version asks for one list of "gifts when the
first of you dies" and one list of "gifts after both of you have died" -- whichever of you dies
first, the same first-death gifts apply. The "Specific Gifts" article itself only appears in your
document at all if you actually add a gift to one of these two lists; otherwise it's left out
entirely, the same way any other unused section is.

RESIDUARY BENEFICIARIES ARE ONE SHARED LIST
Your source document lets you name different final beneficiaries for the Survivor's Trust versus
the Family Trust. This version uses one shared beneficiary list for both, on the view that most
couples want the same people to ultimately receive everything. Tell us if you'd like the two
distributions to name different people or percentages.

THE SURVIVOR'S TRUST AND FAMILY TRUST SHARE ONE FINAL-DISTRIBUTION SECTION
Because both trusts already distribute to the same beneficiary list above, your source document's
separate Distribution / Survivorship / Failure-of-Distribution / Termination sections for each
trust were nearly word-for-word duplicates of each other. This version states that rule once --
at the surviving Trustmaker's death, the Trustee combines what's left in both trusts and
distributes the combined balance to your named beneficiaries -- instead of describing the same
split twice. The outcome for your beneficiaries is identical either way; this only removes the
repetition. This one section lives under "Death of Second Trustmaker," right after "Completion
and Distribution." (This was part of an effort to bring the joint trust closer to your 30-page
target -- even after this change it still runs longer than that; see the conversation for the
exact page count and what else would need to change to get under 30.)

SCHEDULE A (your trust's property list) MATCHES YOUR SOURCE DOCUMENT'S OWN COLUMNS
Unlike the single trust (which trimmed Schedule A down from thirteen categories to five), your
joint trust's own Schedule A already only lists five categories with three columns each. This
version asks the same five: real estate, bank and credit union accounts, investment and
brokerage accounts, business interests, and general household items, plus the same catch-all
"additional property" section. The real estate "Description" column (a deed or legal-description
reference) isn't collected as a separate question and shows as a bracketed placeholder to fill in
by hand, the same way a few little-used fields work in the single trust.

THREE PROBLEMS FOUND AND FIXED
1. Six sentences had a cross-reference to another Section or Article that resolved to the
   generic words "this Trust Agreement" instead of an actual reference -- readable in isolation,
   but grammatically broken where the sentence already said "Section" or "Article" right before
   it (e.g. "...as a child of that Trustmaker in Section this Trust Agreement"). Fixed by pointing
   each one at the actual section or article, so it now reads correctly (e.g. "...under this
   Article" for a same-article reference, or "...under Section 5.03" for a cross-article one).
2. The entire "Specific Gifts" article was wired to never appear, regardless of whether you
   actually added any specific gifts -- a leftover from working out how the two gift lists (first
   death / second death) should nest inside it. Fixed so the article appears only when you've
   actually added a gift to one of the two lists, and each of the two sections inside it
   (first-death gifts, second-death gifts) appears independently, matching whichever list you
   used.
3. Two definitions ("Beneficiary Trust" and "Companion Animal Trust") and one sentence
   mentioning a continuing "Beneficiary Trust" were left over from before those features were cut
   -- defining or mentioning something that no longer appears anywhere else in the document. All
   three are removed. (Also found again, and fixed the same way as before: a text-extraction
   regex bug that can mistake a tab character or an unrelated formatting tag for the start of
   real paragraph text. It showed up once in this document, in a heading that read "Section
   15.01Severability" with no space -- fixed, and rechecked against every other document already
   on this site to confirm nothing else was affected.)

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Whether this is a new trust or a restatement of one you already signed, both Trustmakers' names,
your marital status, children, anyone intentionally excluded, your successor trustee(s) (one at a
time, or together as Co-Trustees), specific gifts at the first and second death, who gets
everything else and in what percentages, and the Schedule A property categories above.
Definitions, trustee powers, and the administration articles are the same for everyone and aren't
asked about. There's no power-of-attorney-agent question here (unlike the single trust) --
nothing in your source document lets an agent under a power of attorney amend this trust, so
there was nothing to ask about.

YOUR TRUST FUNDING GUIDE
The signing-instructions screen now also includes a full Trust Funding Guide -- one section per
common asset type, each with the typical process for moving it into the trust and a blank tracker
to fill in by hand. It's shared with the single trust (see will/NOTES-ON-YOUR-TRUST.txt for the
full explanation, including a simplification worth knowing about) and shows the same way here.

HOW TO EDIT
File: will/trust-joint-template.js  the joint trust document itself (settings + the full text)
File: will/trust-state-text.js      the shared state-by-state signing page (also used by the
                                     single trust; the is_joint flag switches "Trustmaker" wording
                                     to "Trustmakers" for Florida and Delaware's witness pages)
File: will/trust-signing.js         the signing steps shown after review, plus the Trust Funding
                                     Guide (shared with the single trust)
The notes at the top of each file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[each residuary]], and so on).
