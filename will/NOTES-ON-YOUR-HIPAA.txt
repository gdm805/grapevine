NOTES ON YOUR HIPAA AUTHORIZATION (51 state documents)
==========================================================

WHERE THE WORDS CAME FROM
Your "05_HIPAA/FINAL DOCUMENT FOR CODER" folder has a national baseline plus one controlled document per
state. As with the health care directive, I also found an existing prototype at
"CODER/LAST WILL PACKAGE/Grapevine_Last_Will_and_Testament_Package_V27/powers_engine/hipaa_templates.js"
that already had all 51 documents converted into paragraph-by-paragraph data. I checked it against your
actual .docx files for every single state, paragraph by paragraph (not just a sample this time, since the
documents are short enough to check all 51 in full) -- 30 of the 51 matched word for word. The other 21
matched everywhere except one paragraph: the section naming who can receive your information. For those 21
states, the prototype had a generic version of that paragraph; your actual controlled documents tie it
specifically to your health care agent (with an address, phone/email, and up to two backups), so I used
your real document's wording instead of the prototype's for that one paragraph, in all 21 states. Every
other word, in every state, is exactly what your controlled documents have.

TWO DIFFERENT DOCUMENT STYLES -- NOT AN ERROR, JUST HOW YOUR DOCUMENTS WERE DRAFTED
Your 51 states actually use two different document designs:
  30 states (the "Roman-numeral" style, like Alabama) -- closely follows your national baseline's own
             structure and section numbering (I. AUTHORIZATION, II. ..., and so on), lets someone list any
             number of named recipients or classes of recipients, and separately asks about psychotherapy
             notes and substance-use-disorder records (42 C.F.R. Part 2) the way the baseline does.
  21 states (the "plain-language" style, like Texas) -- a shorter, more plainly worded document that ties
             disclosure to your health care agent by name, address and phone/email (plus up to two backup
             agents), asks your date of birth and address directly, and ends with a citation to that
             state's specific statutes.
The questionnaire adjusts itself automatically to whichever style your state uses -- you don't see a
question about which style it is, you just see the right questions for your state.

NO STATE REQUIRES A WITNESS OR NOTARY
I checked the closing lines of all 51 documents specifically for this. Every one of them either says so
directly (like Alabama: "Alabama law does not impose a separate witness or notarization requirement...")
or simply has no witness/notary section at all. Federal HIPAA law doesn't require one, and apparently none
of your 51 state documents add one either. Signing instructions reflect this: sign and date it, no witness
or notary needed, anywhere.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Every state asks: your name, who can receive your information (a list of recipients, or your health care
agent and backups, depending on your state's style), and whether someone will sign on your behalf as your
legally authorized representative. The 30 "Roman-numeral" states also ask about two federally-protected
record types (psychotherapy notes, and substance-use-disorder records under 42 C.F.R. Part 2, including
Part 2's own separate question about SUD counseling notes) and how long the authorization should last
(a specific date, a specific event, or for your lifetime and however long is needed to settle your
affairs). The 21 "plain-language" states instead ask for an optional free-text list of limitations and an
optional expiration date or event, since their documents are written to leave that open-ended.
A handful of states ask one extra question because their own document does:
  California, Connecticut, Delaware, and 19 other "Roman-numeral" states -- whether to include HIV/STI
             information, and/or mental-health records, whichever your specific state's document asks about
             (not every one of the 30 asks about both -- it depends on that state's own document).
  Delaware -- also asks about genetic information specifically, with a place to describe it.
  Maine and Massachusetts -- each have their own separate, additionally-worded HIV authorization with its
             own recipient and purpose fields, asked only when you say yes to including HIV/STI information.

HOW TO EDIT
Folder: will/hipaa/            the full text of each state's authorization, one file per state
File:   will/hipaa-template.js the "sample" watermark switch (there's no shared body text to edit here --
                                every state's document is different from the first line)
File:   will/hipaa-signing.js  the signing steps (the same for every state: no witness or notary needed)
The notes at the top of each state file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[each recipients]], and so on).
