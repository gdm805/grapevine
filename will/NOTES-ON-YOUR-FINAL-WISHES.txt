NOTES ON YOUR FINAL WISHES
==========================

WHAT THIS IS
Final Wishes states what someone wants done with their body and any funeral, memorial, or
personal arrangements, and -- to the extent their state allows it -- names who's authorized to
carry those wishes out. It doesn't distribute property and doesn't replace a will or trust.

WHY THIS ONE HAS FULL 51-STATE TEXT (unlike your Certification of Trust, Affidavit of Trustee, and
Assignment of Personal Property, which all use one shared body with only a notary block or nothing
varying by state)
This document is genuinely different state to state, not just in formality but in substance. I
read your national baseline plus all 51 of your controlled Final_Wishes_<State>.docx files.
Articles One through Five -- the actual wishes and preferences -- are word-for-word identical
across every state file, so those are one shared body. But how a state lets someone name a person
to control disposition, and what it takes to sign that appointment, varies for real:
  - Some states need nothing beyond a signature (Wyoming, North Dakota, District of Columbia).
  - Some need one witness (Maryland, Washington) or two (Arkansas, Connecticut, North Carolina,
    Oklahoma, New York).
  - Some need a notary instead (most other states), and a few let you choose between a witness or
    a notary -- your own controlled files already picked the notary route in every case where a
    choice existed, so this site follows that same choice rather than introducing a new one.
  - A few states need real combinations: New Jersey wants two witnesses AND a notary on its own
    board-approved form; Kentucky wants two witnesses AND a notary; Texas and Rhode Island add a
    separate signed acceptance from the person you name; Montana genuinely needs two different
    execution blocks at once (a notarized designation plus two witnesses for the written
    directions) because it treats those as two different things under its own law.
  - Indiana, Iowa, and Kentucky don't just need different formalities -- their own law wants a
    second, separate declaration. Those three render a full second document (its own heading,
    its own signature line, its own witnesses/notary) right inside the same page, exactly as your
    own controlled files for those states do it.
  - Georgia, Idaho, Kansas, Missouri, Tennessee, Vermont, and West Virginia route the actual
    *authority* to decide through a health care agent or advance directive appointed elsewhere
    (an existing Grapevine document) rather than through this one. Georgia's file only adds its
    own separate notarized affidavit if you name someone different from your health care agent --
    this site reproduces that same conditional, keyed off whether you named anyone here.
So, taken word for word from each state's own controlled file: will/finalwishes-state-text.js.

TWO STATES AREN'T OFFERED
Your own source material -- not my judgment call -- flags Mississippi and South Carolina as
needing a specific state-prescribed form or route ("HARD_STOP" in your coder notes) that isn't
included in what you sent. Rather than guess at wording for those two, this site simply doesn't
offer Final Wishes there yet: choosing either state on the first step shows an explanation and
blocks moving forward, without affecting any other document on the site (will, DPOA, and the rest
are unaffected -- this exclusion lives only in the Final Wishes builder). If you get the missing
state form/route material, say the word and I'll add both states properly.

WHAT'S SIMPLIFIED
  - Texas, Wisconsin, and New York's statutory forms allow an unlimited chain of successor agents
    with individual acceptance signatures for each. This version offers one backup person (an
    "alternate"), matching how every other document on this site handles successors, rather than
    an open-ended list. Say the word if you want a repeating list instead.
  - The big 51-row token-resolution table in your national baseline was cross-checked against your
    controlled state files to confirm which execution route each state actually uses, then
    dropped from the output -- it's coder-only reference material, not customer-facing text.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Your state, your name, your disposition preference (burial, cremation, green/natural burial, body
donation, something else, or no preference) plus an optional place and any additional
instructions, your funeral/memorial/celebration preference plus optional details, optional
personal requests, optional prearranged-or-prepaid-arrangement details and where records are kept,
whether you want to name someone to control disposition (plus an optional backup), and your
signing date.

HOW TO EDIT
File: will/finalwishes-template.js    the shared body (Articles One through Five) plus the
                                      Article Six/Seven wrapper
File: will/finalwishes-state-text.js  the state-specific routing note and execution formalities,
                                      taken word for word from each state's own controlled file
File: will/finalwishes-signing.js     the signing steps shown after review
The notes at the top of each file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[include]], and so on).
