NOTES ON YOUR REVOCABLE LIVING TRUST (single trustmaker)
============================================================

WHAT THIS IS
This is the single-trustmaker route from your file
Grapevine_Coder_Ready_V12_Single_Trustmaker_RLT_Married_Partner_Family_Trust.docx -- for one person
creating a trust on their own, married or not. You said the joint trust (for a married couple
creating one trust together) comes next; this covers only the single-trustmaker version.

HOW THIS DOCUMENT IS BUILT, DIFFERENT FROM YOUR OTHER DOCUMENTS
Your will, power of attorney, health care directive, and HIPAA authorization each need real
state-by-state differences in the body of the document itself, so those use one full file per
state. Your trust document isn't like that: the trust itself reads the same everywhere ("Governed
by the laws of ___"), and the only thing that actually changes by state is the very last page --
how it gets signed. So the trust uses one shared file for the document
(will/trust-template.js) plus one file listing every state's signing page (will/trust-state-text.js),
taken word for word from your NATIONAL EXECUTION LIBRARY folder. Florida and Delaware need two
witnesses in addition to a notary; every other state and DC need a notary only.

YOU ASKED FOR 25 PAGES OR LESS -- WHAT'S IN, AND WHAT'S LEFT OUT
Your source document runs long because it includes several advanced, optional features that a
lot of trusts never use. To keep this to a reasonable length, this first version includes the
core trust -- trust creation, family, successor trustee, trustee powers, what happens if you
become incapacitated, specific gifts, who gets everything else, and a Schedule A property list --
and leaves out four advanced pieces entirely, so they take up no pages unless you ask for them
back:
  - The Family Trust article (for splitting a married couple's combined trust into a protected
    share at the first spouse's death). Your document itself says this only applies "in the
    applicable Trust architecture" -- it's really a joint-trust feature, so it belongs in the
    joint trust more than here.
  - The Companion Animal Trust (a dedicated sub-trust just for a pet).
  - The Charitable Gifts sub-section (special handling when a named charity no longer exists).
  - The Beneficiary Trust option for "everything else" -- holding a beneficiary's share in trust
    with staggered ages instead of paying it outright. Right now, everyone named for the rest of
    your trust receives their share outright, the way your will already works.
Everything else from your document is here, in full -- nothing else was shortened or summarized.
Say the word if you'd like any of these four added back in, either for everyone or as an optional
question the way your health care directive handles its state-specific extras.

SCHEDULE A (your trust's property list) IS ALSO SIMPLIFIED FOR NOW
Your source document's Schedule A has thirteen possible categories of property. This version asks
about the five most common ones -- real estate, bank and credit union accounts, investment and
brokerage accounts, business interests, and general household items -- plus a catch-all
"additional property" section every version keeps. Left out for now: promissory notes, intellectual
property, digital property, titled personal property (like vehicles), an explicit exclusions list,
and beneficiary-designation property (retirement accounts, life insurance, and the like, which your
document is careful to say aren't moved into a trust just by being listed anyway). Add any of these
back in the same way as the ones already there.

YOUR MASTER PACKAGE SPEC DESCRIBES MORE THAN JUST THIS DOCUMENT
Grapevine_Trust_Package_Master_Coder_Spec.docx describes a much bigger package around the trust
itself -- a dynamic funding guide, a funding checklist, and generated transfer letters for banks,
brokerages, and other institutions. None of that is built yet. This pass is the trust document
itself (plus its property schedule); the funding-guide ecosystem is a separate, later project.

TWO PROBLEMS FOUND AND FIXED
1. Delaware's notary acknowledgment, in your National Execution Library, reads "State of State of
   Delaware" -- a duplicated "State of." I removed the duplicate; the rest of the wording is
   exactly as your file has it.
2. California's notary acknowledgment file had a formatting quirk from how it was created (a
   paragraph-border element that read as stray text in the underlying file, and a county/date
   written as [[County]] / [[Signing_Date]] fields instead of blanks like every other state).
   The stray text is removed, and since California's own file marks the county and date as fields
   rather than blanks, those two now fill in from your answers -- every other state's notary or
   witness blanks are still left blank for the notary or witnesses to fill in by hand, same as
   your other documents.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Whether this is a new trust or a restatement of one you already signed, your marital status,
children, anyone intentionally excluded, your successor trustee(s) (one at a time, or together as
Co-Trustees), whether a power-of-attorney agent should also be able to amend or revoke your trust
while you're incapacitated (most people say no), specific gifts, who gets everything else and in
what percentages, and the Schedule A property categories above. Definitions, trustee powers, and
the administration articles are the same for everyone and aren't asked about.

YOUR TRUST FUNDING GUIDE
The signing-instructions screen now also includes a Trust Funding Guide -- one section per common
asset type (real estate, bank accounts, investments, retirement accounts, life insurance, business
interests, vehicles, personal property, digital assets, and everything else), each explaining the
typical process for moving that kind of asset into the trust, plus a small blank tracker to fill in
by hand. Your source document for this was written to auto-populate those trackers from a full
funding questionnaire (asking about each real estate parcel, each account, and so on) and to hide
whole sections you didn't need -- that questionnaire doesn't exist on this site, and adding one
would be a significant new intake flow of its own. So every section always shows, and each tracker
is a blank fill-in-by-hand table instead, matching how notary and witness blanks already work
everywhere else on this site. Say the word if you'd like that fuller, answer-driven version built
out later. It always shows for every trust, whether you sign the single or joint version.

HOW TO EDIT
File: will/trust-template.js     the trust document itself (settings + the full text)
File: will/trust-state-text.js   the state-by-state signing page (notary, and witnesses for FL/DE)
File: will/trust-signing.js      the signing steps shown after review, plus the Trust Funding Guide
The notes at the top of each file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[each residuary]], and so on).
