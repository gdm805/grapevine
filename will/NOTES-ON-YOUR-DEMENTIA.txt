NOTES ON YOUR DEMENTIA AND COGNITIVE DECLINE CARE PREFERENCES
================================================================
(from "Grapevine_National_Dementia_Cognitive_Decline_Care_Preferences_V1_Coder_Ready.docx")

WHAT I DID
Articles One through Seven are your baseline's words, checked sentence by sentence. The one change I made
throughout: your baseline speaks of "the Principal" in the third person ("the Principal prefers..."), while
every other Grapevine document speaks as "I" ("I prefer..."). I converted the whole document to "I/my" for
consistency with the will, the pour-over will, and the power of attorney. Nothing else about the sentences
changed. (Your own baseline was already inconsistent about this -- its very last sentence, the signature line,
already says "I sign these Care Preferences..." in first person, so this conversion also fixes that mismatch.)

ARTICLE EIGHT (EXECUTION) IS NOT VERBATIM -- HERE'S WHY
Your baseline gives two routes:
- "Preferred Integrated Route" (Section 8.01): attach this to the person's Advance Health Care Directive,
  and explicitly do NOT add a second signature, witness or notary block for this supplement.
- "Standalone Supplemental Route" (Section 8.02): a separate document with its own state-by-state execution
  rules -- but your baseline only points at a token, [[STATE_EXECUTION:DEMENTIA_CARE_PREFERENCES]], not at
  real words, and includes [[HARD_STOP:UNRESOLVED_STATE_EXECUTION_RULE]]. Your own Section 10.04 guardrail
  says "Never substitute a generic notary, witness, or jurat block for a state-authorized execution block."

Grapevine doesn't have an Advance Health Care Directive builder yet, so neither route can be offered exactly
as written. I used the Integrated route's rule regardless of state: sign, date, and attach it to the
person's health-care directive, with no witness or notary section of its own. The "How to sign" panel tells
them plainly that some states may still want it witnessed or notarized alongside that directive, and points
them to that directive's own instructions or a local attorney. I did not build the standalone,
state-by-state execution route -- that would need real per-state wording, which your baseline doesn't supply
for this document (see "STATE DOCUMENTS YOU ALREADY HAVE" below).

SHORTENED QUESTIONS
Your baseline's Article Nine lists 11 questions (Q-DCP-001 through Q-DCP-011), onto 7 screens here:
- Q-DCP-001 ("would you like to add this?") isn't asked again -- starting this builder already answers it.
- Q-DCP-003, 004, and 005 (three near-identical "goal of care" questions, one per stage) became ONE
  "overall approach" question with three choices -- treat everything, balance treatment with comfort as it
  worsens, or comfort first from the start -- each of which sets all three stages at once. A "set this
  separately" toggle reveals the original three questions for anyone who wants that level of detail.
- Q-DCP-002 (values), 006 (hospital transfer), 007 (infections), 008 (feeding-tube guidance), 009 (care
  setting), 010 (routines/people), and 011 (POLST) are all still here, grouped onto fewer screens by topic.
- Section 5.04 (surgery/intensive care/other burdensome treatment) has body text in your baseline but NO
  numbered question anywhere in Article Nine -- it seems to have been left out of your questionnaire by
  oversight. I added a short optional question for it, next to the similar feeding-tube question, so that
  section has a way to be filled in rather than always being empty.

ONE MORE THING I ADDED: A NON-BLOCKING CAPACITY CHECK
Your Section 10.03 guardrail says: if someone indicates current substantial cognitive impairment or
uncertainty about capacity to sign, show neutral guidance to see a clinician or lawyer -- but don't block
them just for their age or a diagnosis. I added exactly that: a question asking whether they can currently
understand this document and communicate their own wishes, with "Yes" or "I'm not sure." Choosing "I'm not
sure" shows a gentle note suggesting they talk to a doctor or lawyer first, but does not stop them from
continuing, matching your guardrail.

WHAT THIS BUILDER DOESN'T ASK (BY DESIGN, PER YOUR OWN GUARDRAILS)
- No health-care agent is named here. Section 10.02 says this supplement should inherit the agent's name
  from the Advance Health Care Directive, not collect it separately or let someone appoint a different
  agent here. Since there's no AHCD builder yet, the document just refers to "my health-care agent"
  generically -- nothing here names anyone.
- No diagnosis, clinical stage, or capacity determination is asked for, per Section 10.03.
- County isn't asked. Unlike the will and the power of attorney, nothing in this document's text uses a
  county, so I left that question out.

STATE DOCUMENTS YOU ALREADY HAVE (NOT YET USED HERE)
Your "06_DIMENTIA DIRECTIVE/Final Docs for Coder" folder has a Dementia_Directive_<State>.docx for all 51
states. I opened two of them (California and a few others) to understand the design: Articles One through
Seven in every one of those files are identical to the baseline, word for word -- only the EXECUTION section
differs, and even there, most of them just reference a token (like [[STATE_EXECUTION:TX_HCD_WITNESS_ATTESTATION]])
rather than spelling out real witness or notary wording. That real wording appears to live with your
Advance Health Care Directive project (the "04_AHCD" folder, with its own national baseline and per-state
audited documents) and your "NATIONAL EXECUTION LIBRARY" folder, not in the dementia-directive files
themselves. Making this document state-compliant the way the will, the pour-over will, and the power of
attorney are would mean cross-referencing those two other projects to resolve each state's token into real
text -- a real research task, not something to guess at. I did not attempt it. Say the word and I'll take it
on the way I did for the power of attorney's 51 states.
