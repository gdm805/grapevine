NOTES ON YOUR DURABLE POWER OF ATTORNEY  (51 state documents)
=============================================================

WHAT CHANGED
The builder no longer uses the national baseline. Each state now loads its own controlled document from
will/dpoa/ (alabama.js ... wyoming.js), taken word for word from your files Grapevine_DPOA_<State>.docx in
the state_documents folder. That is where each state's notices, witness lines, notary wording and agent
information come from. I wrote none of the state-specific wording.

Source of truth: the "..._V2_4_2_Shared_Incapacity" folder (not the copy named "... 2"; the only difference
between the two is the California file, and its text is identical).

HOW I CHECKED IT
For all 51 states, three ways (right away / only on incapacity with two backups / care-facility "yes") = 153
documents, I rebuilt each one with your own builder's rules (state_templates.js, the special-power suppression,
the shared incapacity language) and compared it word by word with what this site produces. All 153 match.
The only differences are formatting: blank lines are underscores, a colon after "Date", and the state-name
line under the title (for example "TEXAS STATE DPOA") is dropped because the title already names the state.
I also checked that no heading is left with nothing under it and no code is left in the text.

HOW IT FOLLOWS YOUR V2.4 / V2.4.2 RULES
- The governing state's document is loaded; nothing is substituted from another state.
- Residence state, governing state and signing state are separate. The person picks where they live and, if they
  want, a different state's form. The signing county and date are blank lines filled in at the signing.
- Standard DPOA: no special-power questions. Article Seven is kept in each file but switched off, and the
  numbering closes the gap around it: Digital Assets is now Article Seven, and the rest move up to match
  (Third-Party Reliance is Eight, Conservatorship is Nine, General Provisions is Ten). If a state's document
  is ever changed to turn Article Seven back on, the numbering shifts back automatically.
  (This replaces the five "extra power" questions I had added earlier. They are gone.)
- Sections 3.02 and 3.03 appear only for "only on incapacity". Your shared Incapacity and Restoration of Capacity
  paragraphs (in will/dpoa-template.js) come first, then the state's own wording. Florida offers only "right away",
  as your Florida document says, so it skips that question.
- Extra questions appear only where your documents need them: California asks who decides incapacity (only for
  "on incapacity"; blank means the agent); Nevada and Utah ask about a care facility.
- A heading with nothing under it is dropped, like your builder does.

WHAT YOUR DOCUMENTS SAY ABOUT SIGNING (so you can check them, I did not judge the law)
- Witness lines appear in 13 states: Arizona, California, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Illinois,
  Louisiana, Maryland, New York, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Washington. Arizona, Delaware, Georgia and
  Illinois show one witness line; the other nine show two. (Washington and California show them as one of two ways to sign.)
- California, Minnesota, New York, Tennessee, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin and Wyoming give a choice
  or an optional acknowledgment. The "How to sign" box tells the person to use the way that applies.
- Oregon has no notary or witness section at all. Every other state has a notary acknowledgment.
- The "How to sign" box under the document says whether the state's document has a notary section, witness
  lines or a choice. It reads that from the document, so it stays right if you edit a state file.

THINGS I NOTICED IN YOUR DOCUMENTS (please look at these)
1. Alabama is the only document that makes Execution an Article ("Article Twelve, Execution" with sections 12.01
   and 12.02). The other 50 use an unnumbered EXECUTION heading. Alabama also has "[EXECUTION FOLLOWS ON NEXT
   PAGE]", which I kept and turned into a page break.
2. The venue line "DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA" in the D.C. notary block: your builder hides it (it drops
   any line equal to the state name). This site keeps it.
3. Some notices in the documents are in all capitals and are kept as bold paragraphs. Statutory notices are shown
   as printed in your documents. Please confirm type-size or boldface requirements state by state; the PDF and
   Word files use one size for the notices.
4. The agent-information notices ("Important information for agent") appear in your documents but have no agent
   signature line in most states, so the agent never signs. Only some states include an agent acknowledgment.
5. Every document says "of ____ County, <State>" using the person's residence county. Louisiana (parish), Alaska
   (borough) and D.C. have no counties.

FIXED: THE ARTICLE SEVEN GAP
Section 6.03 "Reserved Trust Powers" in all 51 documents said trust powers are "governed by the express-power
provisions of Article Seven," even though Article Seven is switched off. That sentence now reads "...is governed
by the express-power provisions of this Power of Attorney" instead of naming an article that doesn't appear. The
numbering also no longer skips a number: Digital Assets is Article Seven, Third-Party Reliance is Eight,
Conservatorship is Nine, and General Provisions is Ten (checked in the on-screen document, the PDF and the Word
file for several states). Your own builder still shows the old gap (Six to Eight); this site no longer matches
it on that one point, by request.

HOW TO EDIT
Change one state: edit will/dpoa/<state>.js. Change the shared incapacity words: edit will/dpoa-template.js.
The notes at the top of each state file explain the codes ([[if x]], [[elif y]], [[each successors]], [[include x]]).
