NOTES ON YOUR GENERAL ASSIGNMENT OF PERSONAL PROPERTY TO TRUST
================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
A trust only controls property that's actually been retitled into its name. Real estate, cars, and
financial accounts get retitled one at a time, directly with the county, DMV, or institution. But
plenty of what a person owns -- furniture, jewelry, tools, family keepsakes -- has no title or
account to change at all. This Assignment is the backstop: one signed document that moves the
Assignor's interest in that everyday personal property into the trust, without a separate deed or
form for each item.

WHY NO PER-STATE VERSION (unlike your DPOA, HCD, HIPAA authorization, trust, Certification, and
Affidavit of Trustee, which all vary what's required state to state)
Your source file included a state-by-state table covering all 50 states and DC -- 51 rows -- each
resolving an "execution requirement" token. I checked every single row: all 51 resolve to the same
answer, SIGNATURE_ONLY. No state requires a witness, notary, or sworn oath for this particular
document; a plain signature is enough everywhere. Your own file's "Global Token Resolution" and
"Coder Validation Rules" sections say the same thing directly, and flag that generation should stop
and go back to legal review if any state ever resolves to something else. Because there's no actual
state-to-state difference to encode, this document is a single, state-agnostic page -- there's no
will/assignment-state-text.js the way the other trust-support documents have one.

THE OPTIONAL NOTARY PAGE
Nothing in your source material requires notarization here, but a specific bank, brokerage, or
title company might still ask for a notarized signature before they'll rely on it, or someone might
simply want the extra formality. Because that step is optional everywhere rather than a real
statutory requirement varying state to state, this version offers one generic acknowledgment block
(state, blank county/parish line, blank date and signer lines) rather than 51 state-specific ones --
there was no per-state statute to draw each state's exact wording from the way there was for the
Certification of Trust's notary blocks. If a specific recipient wants their own jurisdiction's exact
acknowledgment language, that's worth a quick check with them or an attorney before relying on the
generic version.

WHAT'S LEFT OUT FROM YOUR SOURCE FILE
Your source document included a long "CODER IMPLEMENTATION CONTROLS" section and the 51-row state
table mentioned above -- both written for whoever builds the document, not for the person signing
it, so neither appears in the output. Everything a signer actually reads made it into this version:
the general assignment itself, what it does and doesn't cover (Article Three's exclusions -- real
property, titled vehicles, financial and retirement accounts, beneficiary designations, business
interests, digital assets, and anything else nonassignable), the joint-trust ownership-character
rules, and the execution page.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Your state (for the header and execution line only -- it doesn't change any wording), whether the
trust is single-trustmaker or joint, the trust's name and date (plus whether it's been restated),
the Trustmaker(s)' names, whether the trust is currently revocable, any specific items you want
called out by name in addition to the general assignment, whether to add the optional notary page,
and where and when you'll sign.

HOW TO EDIT
File: will/assignment-template.js   the Assignment document itself (settings + the full text)
File: will/assignment-signing.js    the signing steps shown after review
The notes at the top of each file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[each property]], and so on).
