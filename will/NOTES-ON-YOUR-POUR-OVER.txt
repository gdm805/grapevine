NOTES ON YOUR POUR-OVER WILLS  (Will_Pour_Over_<State>.docx, all 51)
====================================================================

HOW THE 51 STATES WERE HANDLED
I compared every state's document with California. Apart from capitalization, the body of the will is
identical in all 51. Only these things change from state to state, and each is now a setting or a section in
will/state-text.js (one section per state, which you can edit like a Word document):
  1. The community property sentence in Section 6.02.
  2. Florida only: a homestead sentence instead, and no No-Contest Provision.
  3. The execution, witness and notary text at the end. It has 36 different versions across the 51 states.
I checked the result by generating the will for all 51 states in three different situations and confirming
that every fixed line of that state's own document appears in the output. Nothing was missing.

CHANGED (the differences from your documents)
1. Community property. You told me the community property states are Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana,
   Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin. Your documents had the sentence only for California
   ("community or quasi-community property") and Arizona and Idaho ("community property"). I added it for
   the other six, using "community property". Wisconsin's law calls it "marital property", so please check that
   wording. In all nine states I show the sentence only when the person has a spouse or domestic partner.
2. Section headings. 27 of your documents capitalize "And", "Of", "Or" and "In" in headings, and 21 use
   lowercase ("Declaration, Identification, and Capacity"). I made all 51 use lowercase.
3. Sections that print empty in your California draft (Co-Personal Representatives, and the whole Guardian article
   when no child is under 18) now appear only when they apply, so the numbering closes up. References such as
   "Section 5.04" follow the numbers automatically.
4. Definitions run together in one paragraph in your drafts. Same words, one per line.
5. Signing date and county are blank lines to fill in by hand. Everything else in each state's execution
   text is exactly as you wrote it.
6. The printed footer reads "Pour-Over Will For NAME | Page X of Y", like your Word footer.

PROBLEMS IN YOUR SOURCE DOCUMENTS (please look at these)
A. COLORADO. Under "Witness Attestation" there is no attestation text, only signature lines, and the document
   ends with a "Self-Proving Affidavit" heading with nothing under it. I left the empty heading out of the will
   (a note in the Colorado section says so). Colorado is switched on, but please add the missing text.
B. Illinois and Indiana. After each witness's Address line there is an extra "Signature" line. I left it as written.
C. Indiana ends with "No notary is required for this traditional self-proving clause." I kept it. Because of it,
   the signing steps do not tell Indiana people to use a notary.
D. Bracketed placeholders such as "[OFFICIAL SEAL]" and "[Florida certificate fields / seal as applicable]" print
   as plain text under the notary line. Tell me if you want them removed or shown differently.
E. "County" is used everywhere, including Louisiana (parishes), Alaska (boroughs) and the District of Columbia
   (no counties; your document says "County, the District of Columbia"). The county question and Section 1.01
   say "County" for all. I can switch the word per state if you want.
F. All 51 documents use two witnesses. (I had guessed three for Vermont earlier. That was wrong, and I have
   corrected it. Your Vermont document uses two.)

SIGNING STEPS
Each state is marked with whether its document has a notary section, and the "How to sign" steps tell the person
to complete it in front of a notary when it does. 44 states have one. California, Colorado, the District of
Columbia, Illinois, Indiana, Maryland and Ohio do not.

- Property fiduciary: one backup.
