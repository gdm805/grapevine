NOTES ON YOUR AFFIDAVIT OF TRUSTEE
====================================

WHAT THIS IS
An Affidavit of Trustee is a sworn statement -- stronger than a Certification of Trust's simple
notarized acknowledgment -- confirming a Trustee's identity, status, and authority. It's useful for
a specific pending transaction, or when a recipient wants more than a Certification of Trust
provides. Unlike a Certification, it needs a JURAT: the Trustee swears or affirms the statements
are true in front of a notary, rather than the notary simply confirming who signed.

HOW THIS DOCUMENT IS BUILT
Same architecture as your Certification of Trust and both living trusts: one shared, state-agnostic
body, with only the state-specific execution page swapped in. Here that's justified even more
strongly than for the Certification -- your 51 state files for this document are, with rare
exceptions, word-for-word identical to your national baseline. The ONLY thing that genuinely varies
by state is the jurat itself (the sworn-oath wording, and whether the state calls the local
subdivision a "county" or, in Louisiana's case, a "Parish"). That per-state jurat is in
will/affidavit-state-text.js, taken word for word from each state's own controlled file.

THIS DOCUMENT COVERS BOTH YOUR SINGLE AND JOINT TRUST
Your source document is written to work with either a single-trustmaker trust or a joint one, so
this builder asks up front which kind of trust the Affidavit concerns, then adjusts the questions:
  - Single trust: whether the Trustmaker is living or deceased.
  - Joint trust: whether both Trustmakers are living, one has died (and if so, whether this
    Affidavit concerns the Survivor's Trust, the Family Trust, or something else), or both have died.

WHAT'S SIMPLIFIED COMPARED TO YOUR SOURCE FILE
Your baseline document includes an "Article Seven -- State-Specific Routing" section instructing
the preparer to STOP and use a different, Michigan- or Nebraska-specific statutory certificate
instead of this national Affidavit, when the purpose is simply to establish the trust's existence
or the Trustee's authority. I checked: Michigan's and Nebraska's own actual delivered state files
don't implement that alternate route -- they use the same national Affidavit body as every other
state, just with their own jurat. This version does the same, and leaves that routing note out
entirely (it was coder-only guidance, never rendered).
Also left out: the per-Trustee "capacity" label seen in your Certification of Trust (whether
someone is an original vs. successor Trustee) -- every currently acting Trustee is just listed as
"Trustee" here too, for the same reason (keeps the question list short; it's the Affiant's own
successor status, asked separately, that actually matters to this document).

THE JURAT: SAME CONVENTION AS YOUR OTHER DOCUMENTS, WITH ONE DIFFERENCE
The execution statement and signature line (the first few lines of each state's block -- "Executed
in [city], [county] County, [state], on [date]," then the signature) are filled in from your
answers, the same as every document's own signature block. Everything in the jurat itself that the
notary has to witness in person (county, date, the Affiant's name as the notary verifies it) is left
blank for hand-fill at the signing appointment -- a notary won't accept a pre-filled jurat. Florida's
checkbox-style physical-presence/personally-known options are rendered the same way as your
Certification of Trust's Florida block. Louisiana keeps its own civil-law terminology (Parish, not
County) throughout, matching the rest of the site's treatment of Louisiana.

WHAT THE QUESTIONS COVER
Whether the trust is single or joint, the trust's name and dates, the Trustmaker(s) and their
living/deceased status (or, for a joint trust after the first death, which trust or share this
Affidavit concerns), the Affiant (you) and whether you're a successor Trustee, the currently acting
Trustee(s) and how they act together if there's more than one, an optional specific transaction and
any restriction on it, and where you'll sign.

HOW TO EDIT
File: will/affidavit-template.js    the Affidavit itself (settings + the full text)
File: will/affidavit-state-text.js  the state-by-state jurat (execution statement + sworn oath)
File: will/affidavit-signing.js     the signing steps shown after review
The notes at the top of each file explain the codes used ([[if x]], [[each trustees]], and so on).
