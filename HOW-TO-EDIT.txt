HOW TO EDIT YOUR WEBSITE  (no coding needed)
===========================================

You can change the important things by editing plain text files with any text editor
(TextEdit on a Mac in "Plain Text" mode, or Notepad on Windows). Do not use Word.
After you edit a file, save it and refresh the page in your browser.

Never delete the lines at the very top and bottom of these files. They begin with
"window." and end with a backtick and a semicolon. Everything between them is yours.
Never type the backtick key ( on the same key as ~ ) inside the text.


1. THE WILL
-----------
File: will/will-template.js
This is the full text of the will. Change the words like you would in a document.
The notes at the top of the file explain the few special codes, such as:

   {{name}}                                  a blank that fills in from the person's answers
   [[if has_children]] ... [[else]] ... [[end]]   text that appears only in some cases
   # Title                                   an Article (ARTICLE ONE, TWO...) - numbers are automatic
   ## Title                                  a Section (Section 1.01, 1.02...) - numbers are automatic
   [[ref residuary]]                         writes the number of a heading you labelled {#residuary},
                                             like "Article Six", and stays correct if numbers move

TO REMOVE THE "SAMPLE - NOT FOR SIGNING" WATERMARK AND THE GREEN NOTICE BAR:
   near the top of will/will-template.js, change   draft_watermark = yes   to   no
The will text now uses the wording from your own will (last will - unformatted). Where I added
or changed anything, a note in the file says ADDED or CHANGED, and will/NOTES-ON-YOUR-DRAFT.txt
lists everything. Do not switch the watermark off until you are satisfied with every word.

File: will/signing-instructions.js   the "How to sign your will" steps shown at the end.

                                     notary text, community property, and Florida's differences.

The questions themselves (what is asked, in what order) live in js/will.js. Ask me to change
those and I will.

3. PRICES
---------
File: js/plans.js  (the numbers at the very top, under "prices"). Every plan now has TWO prices --
"single" (one person) and "couple" (a married couple or domestic partners completing their
documents together, who generally need two of most documents, hence the higher price) -- for
example: will: { single: 119, couple: 199 }. Both the pricing page and the checkout page read this
file, so a change here shows up in both places automatically. If you change what a plan includes,
also update the matching <ul class="includes"> list in pricing.html (inside each plan's collapsible
"What's included" section) so the wording matches -- these are written out in full there and in
js/plans.js, on purpose: no plan's list should ever end in "...and more."

THE SINGLE/COUPLE TOGGLE: pricing.html has one toggle near the top of the plan cards ("Just me" /
"Me and my spouse or partner") that updates all three prices at once. The choice is remembered in
the visitor's browser for that session (sessionStorage, not a permanent cookie) and carried into
checkout.html and paid.html automatically, so the price stays consistent through the whole flow
even though it's never written into the URL by hand. Read a price anywhere in the code with
GV_PLANS.priceFor('essentials', 'couple') rather than reaching into .prices directly.


3b. PAYMENT (checkout.html and paid.html)
------------------------------------------
People can answer every question and preview their document for free. Paying happens only when
they try to download, print, or go to checkout directly from the pricing page. This is set up so
you can turn on real payment yourself, with no coding:

File: js/checkout.js   the one place to paste your Stripe Payment Links (a Stripe Payment Link is
                       a ready-made, Stripe-hosted payment page). Because every plan now has a
                       single and a couple price, you need SIX Payment Links, not three -- one per
                       plan per household size, each at the matching price in js/plans.js. The
                       instructions for exactly what to paste, and where in Stripe to set the
                       "after payment" redirect (which now needs to carry &household=single or
                       &household=couple, in addition to the plan), are in the notes at the top of
                       that file.
File: js/entitlements.js   remembers, in the visitor's own browser, which plan they paid for and
                       which documents that unlocks. This is a browser check, not an account
                       system - good enough to stop casual downloading without paying, but not
                       something a technical visitor couldn't get around. A real lock that nobody
                       can bypass needs a small server, which is a later stage for this project.
                       Entitlements aren't split by household size -- paying for a plan unlocks
                       the same documents whether you paid the single or couple price.
File: checkout.html    the payment page itself (order summary, "Continue to secure payment").
File: paid.html        the page Stripe sends someone back to right after they pay. It unlocks the
                       plan in their browser and sends them on to their document.

TO TEST WITHOUT SETTING UP STRIPE: open paid.html?plan=will in your browser (or essentials, or
complete; add &household=couple to test that price instead of single). It unlocks that plan in
your browser exactly like a real payment would, so you can see how the Download and Print buttons
behave once someone has paid. Clear it again with your browser's "clear site data," or open the
page in a private window.

WHICH PLAN UNLOCKS WHICH DOCUMENT: also set in js/plans.js, under "unlocks." Right now: Will
unlocks the will, Important Contacts and Final Wishes; Essentials adds the power of attorney,
health care directive, dementia care preferences and HIPAA authorization; Complete adds the
pour-over will, the plain will, the living trust (single or joint), the Certification of Trust,
Affidavit of Trustee, and the General Assignment of Personal Property to Trust. Change these lists
as you add more documents. This list is independent of household size -- it doesn't change based
on whether someone paid the single or couple price.


3c. THE ESSENTIALS/COMPLETE PACKAGE FLOW
------------------------------------------
Essentials and Complete are more than one document. Someone who clicks "Start with Essentials" or
"Start with Complete" now answers every document in that plan back to back, instead of having to
find each builder on their own: finish the will (or, for Complete, the pour-over will) and a
"Continue to your Power of Attorney" button appears in place of the usual download buttons; finish
that and it continues to the Health Care Directive, then Dementia Care Preferences, then the HIPAA
Authorization. A small track at the top of the questions ("Essentials package -- document 2 of 5")
shows where they are, and every document name in it is a link, so they can jump back to review or
change an earlier one at any time. Downloading and printing stay locked, the same as always, until
they pay -- but now that only happens once, at the LAST document, where a "Your package is
complete" screen lists every document with a link to open it. Pay once there and every document in
the plan unlocks together.

File: js/flow.js   the only file this feature needs. FLOWS near the top lists, for each plan, which
                   documents it walks through and in what order -- right now Essentials is
                   Will, Power of Attorney, Health Care Directive, Dementia Care Preferences, HIPAA
                   Authorization, and Complete is the same with the Pour-Over Will in place of the
                   Will. The trust, final wishes and important contacts are listed on the pricing
                   page but don't have builders yet, so they aren't part of this walk-through --
                   add them to FLOWS once they do.
The rest of the feature lives inside js/will.js (the package track, the "Continue to your..."
button, and the final "package is complete" screen) and needs no editing. Someone who opens a
single builder directly -- typing dpoa.html, or using the "Or start with your..." links on the
pricing page -- still gets that one document on its own, exactly as before; nothing changes unless
they came from a "Start with Essentials/Complete" button.

5. DOWNLOADS (PDF and Word)
---------------------------
The "Download PDF" and "Download for Word" buttons build the files inside the visitor's browser. Both put
"Page X of Y" at the bottom of every page, and the running footer reads "Last Will and Testament of NAME"
or "Pour-Over Will For NAME". While the sample watermark is on, both files carry it too.
The Word file is a real .docx that you can edit. The footer's page numbers are Word fields, so they stay
correct as you edit. Nothing about these files needs editing. The code is in js/will-export.js, and js/vendor/
holds two libraries it uses (see js/vendor/README.txt).


6. SEE YOUR CHANGES
-------------------
Double-click a page such as will.html or index.html to open it in your browser. It works
straight from your computer. To put it on the internet, upload the whole folder to a website host.
Keep the folders exactly as they are.


7. WHAT HAPPENS TO PEOPLE'S ANSWERS, AND TO PAYMENT?
-----------------------------------------------------
Answers are saved only in the visitor's own web browser. Nothing is sent to you or to any server.
Payment (once you turn it on - see section 3b) is handled entirely by Stripe on Stripe's own page;
you never see or store card details, and neither does this site. Whether someone has paid is also
remembered only in their own browser, not in any account, so there's no "my purchases" page yet,
and someone who switches devices or clears their browser needs their email receipt if they want
you to unlock it for them by hand. A real account system - so payment, answers and documents all
follow the person instead of the browser - needs a small server, which is a later stage.

SINGLE DOCUMENTS AND SHORT PACKAGES ("Just need one document?")
================================================================
The pricing page has a section (id="single") that sells two short packages and nine single documents:
  - Health Care Package: Health Care Directive, Dementia Care Preferences, HIPAA, Final Wishes
  - Trust Paperwork: Schedule A, Certification of Trust, Affidavit of Trustee, Assignment of Property
  - Any single document: Power of Attorney, Health Care Directive, Dementia, HIPAA, Final Wishes,
    Schedule A, Certification, Affidavit, Assignment.
Prices live in js/plans.js (prices: doc, health, trustpaper). A couple gets two copies of the personal
documents (one per person, the second can be a "mirror image" of the first); trust documents are one per trust.
Stripe links: js/checkout.js, PAYMENT_LINKS, entries doc / health / trustpaper (single and couple).
Every single document shares the "doc" link; the checkout page remembers which document was chosen and
paid.html unlocks just that one. Schedule A is its own document now (schedulea.html, will/schedulea-*.js).
