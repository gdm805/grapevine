Documents

The essentials, each in plain words

Most families start with a will and add the others as life gets more complicated.

  • Last Will and TestamentSay who receives what you own, who carries out your wishes, and who raises your children. ChildrenGuardianExecutor
  • Revocable Living TrustHold your home and savings in a trust so they pass to your family privately, without going through probate. HomePrivacyProbate
  • Durable Power of AttorneyChoose someone you trust to pay bills and handle paperwork if you can’t. MoneyPaperwork
  • Health Care DirectivePut your medical wishes on record and name the person who speaks for you when you can’t. MedicalWishes

Plain language

Legal words are exact. Understanding them shouldn’t require a law degree.

Every question you answer is in plain English — here’s how one of those answers becomes real legal wording. Flip the switch to compare.

Last Will and Testament

Article II · Guardian

Should it become necessary for a guardian of the person of any minor child of mine to be appointed, I hereby nominate and appoint my sister, Lucía Alvarez, to serve as such guardian, and should she fail or cease to act for any reason, I nominate my friend, Priya Nair, as successor guardian.

If I can’t look after my children, I want my sister, Lucía Alvarez, to raise them. If she can’t, I choose my friend, Priya Nair.

Same meaning, different words.

    Questions

    Things people ask before they start

    Is Grapevine a law firm?

    No. Grapevine provides document preparation tools and general information, not legal advice. We can’t represent you or act as your attorney.

    Do I still need a lawyer?

    Many people with simple situations don’t. If your family, business or property is complicated, or if you’re unsure, talk to a licensed attorney in your state.

    Does my will need witnesses?

    Usually, yes. Signing rules vary by state, and most require a will to be signed in front of witnesses. Check your state’s requirements before you sign.

    How often should I review my documents?

    Look them over every few years, and whenever something big changes: a marriage or divorce, a new child, a move, or a major change in what you own.

    Where should I keep my signed documents?

    Somewhere safe that the right people can reach. Tell your executor where they are, and give copies to anyone named in a power of attorney or healthcare directive.
