About Grapevine

Before you start

Is Grapevine a law firm?

No. Grapevine provides document preparation tools and general information, not legal advice. We can’t represent you or act as your attorney.

Do I still need a lawyer?

Many people with simple situations don’t. If your family, business or property is complicated, or if you’re unsure, talk to a licensed attorney in your state.

What if my situation is complicated?

If you own a business, have a blended family, or expect a large estate, talk to a licensed attorney in your state before you decide. A lawyer’s advice may be worth more than the price of any document here.

Where are my answers saved?

In your own web browser, on your device. They aren’t sent to or stored on our servers. On a shared computer, use “Start over” when you’re finished.

Pricing and payment

Before you buy

Do I have to pay before I start?

No. Answering the questions and previewing your document is free. You pay only when you’re ready to download or print it.

Is this a subscription?

No. Every price is one-time.

Are there other costs?

Possibly. Some states or counties charge separate fees, such as a notary for certain documents or a recording fee when a home is moved into a trust. Those go to the state or notary, not to Grapevine, and they vary by location.

Does the price include legal advice?

No. Grapevine is not a law firm. We provide document preparation tools and general information, not legal advice.

Can I get a refund?

Your documents are delivered the moment you pay, so sales are final, except for a duplicate charge, a wrong amount, or a problem on our side that stops you downloading. Email us within 30 days with your receipt. See our Terms.

I changed computers. How do I get my documents back?

Your purchase is remembered in the browser you used. Email support@grapevinedocs.com with your payment receipt and we’ll help you get access again. Always save a copy of your downloaded documents.

Signing and after

Once your documents are ready

Does my will need witnesses?

Usually, yes. Signing rules vary by state, and most require a will to be signed in front of witnesses. Each document comes with signing steps for your state; check them before you sign.

Does a trust move my house into it automatically?

No. A trust and its Schedule A list what belongs in the trust, but real estate usually needs a new deed, and accounts usually need to be retitled or given a beneficiary.

Where should I keep my signed documents?

Somewhere safe that the right people can reach. Tell your executor where they are, and give copies to anyone named in a power of attorney or health care directive.

How often should I review my documents?

Look them over every few years, and whenever something big changes: a marriage or divorce, a new child, a move, or a major change in what you own.
