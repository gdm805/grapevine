Power of attorney and health care directive: what's the difference?

September 17, 2026 · 1 min read

A will only takes effect after you die. Two other documents help if you become unable to make decisions while you are alive: a power of attorney and a health care directive.

Durable power of attorney

A power of attorney lets you name someone, called your agent, to handle your financial and legal affairs. This might include paying bills, managing accounts, filing taxes or dealing with property. A "durable" power of attorney stays in effect if you become incapacitated, which is when it is most needed. It ends when you die, at which point your executor takes over.

Health care directive

A health care directive covers medical decisions. It usually has two parts:

  • A health care agent. This is the person you name to make medical decisions for you if you can't.
  • Your treatment wishes. This is sometimes called a living will. It records your preferences about things like life support and pain relief.

HIPAA authorization

Privacy law limits who can see your medical records. A HIPAA authorization names the people allowed to receive your health information, so your agent and family can talk to your doctors.

Without these documents

Without these documents, your family may have to ask a court to appoint a guardian or conservator to make decisions for you. That process can be slow, expensive and public.

Agents and backups

The agent under each document can be the same person or different people, and each document can name backups.

Requirements for these documents, including how they are signed, differ by state.
