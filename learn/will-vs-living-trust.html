Will or living trust: how they differ

September 21, 2026 · 2 min read

Both a will and a living trust let you decide who receives your property. They work differently, and some people have both.

The short answer

Property that passes under a will generally goes through probate, a court process with filings, deadlines and fees, and the court file is generally open to the public. Property held in a funded living trust is generally administered by the trustee you chose without going to court. How long either takes, and what it costs, depends on the estate and on the state's law; some states have simpler procedures for small estates.

The main differences

  • Probate. Property in a will generally goes through probate, a court process that can take months and is usually public. Property held in a living trust generally passes without probate.
  • Privacy. A will becomes part of the public record once it is filed with the court. A trust usually stays private.
  • If you become unable to manage your affairs. A will only takes effect after you die. A living trust can name someone to manage trust property while you are still alive but unable to.
  • Cost and effort. A will is typically simpler and less expensive to create. A trust takes more work, because you must transfer your property into it, such as retitling a house.

Funding a trust

A trust only controls property that has actually been placed in it. A trust that is created but never funded may do little. A pour-over will works with a trust: it sends anything left outside the trust into it at death.

How these differences apply depends on your family, your property and your state's law. If you have any legal questions about your particular circumstances we recommend that you seek a qualified attorney to assist you.
