What happens if you die without a will?

September 20, 2026 · 1 min read

When someone dies without a valid will, they are said to have died "intestate." The law of their state then decides what happens to their property.

Who inherits

Each state has a formula for this, called the intestacy statute. In general, it favors close relatives in a set order, often starting with a spouse and children, then parents, siblings and more distant relatives. The details vary a great deal by state. For example, the share a spouse receives can depend on whether there are children, and whether the children are also the spouse's.

Who is left out

State formulas generally do not include people the law doesn't consider family. That can mean:

  • an unmarried partner
  • a close friend
  • a stepchild who was never legally adopted
  • a charity you care about

Who raises your children

If you have children under 18, and no other parent is able to care for them, a court decides who becomes their guardian. The court will try to act in the children's best interests, but it will not know your wishes unless you have written them down.

Who handles the estate

A court will appoint someone, often called an administrator, to settle your affairs. They may be a person you would not have chosen, and they may need to post a bond, which adds cost.

What a will changes

A will lets you choose who receives your property, name your executor, and nominate a guardian for your children. To see what a will involves, read What is a will?
